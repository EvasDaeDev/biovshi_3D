# P1 — terminal physics ownership

LabSimulationCore.incapacitate(id) is an idempotent terminal transition, emits one copied fighter-incapacitated event with the current attached geometry, and stops all self-propelled movement and procedural gait. It keeps fighter identity and segment IDs for later match results. setIncapacitatedPose accepts ordered plain geometry from the physics adapter and validates identity, dimensions and finiteness. Falling physics-owned actors are fixed obstacles in the kinematic solver; settled actors are excluded from capsule collisions. Snapshots render the same actor meshes from the external physical pose, with no duplicate procedural body.

Rapier IncapacitatedBodies creates a single dynamic compound rigid body per patient using all surviving capsules and spheres. It preserves the pose at handoff and falls under gravity with a small initial angular perturbation. Corpse-floor, wall, debris, mound and corpse collisions use shared world groups; after settling, the dynamic capsule compound is removed and replaced with up to four fixed walkable cones (head/neck, chest/abdomen/arms, pelvis/upper legs, lower legs/feet), based on the actual retained geometry. Visual anatomy remains unchanged; cones are invisible except in debug mode. Heights are capped at .30 m and combined with debris by maximum, not sum. Reset frees the common world. This is deliberately NOT an articulated ragdoll: joints remain fixed in the final pose, living patients step across settled bodies via the existing terrain height and IK, rather than being blocked by anatomical capsules. They do not apply physical impulses to falling bodies. The isolated corpse scenario exercises the terminal transition without inventing HP rules. Actual combat incapacitation/oil/trauma triggers belong to P2/P3. Existing living movement unchanged. Tests cover terminal state, physics pose ownership, compound fall, terrain conversion, reset and immovable obstacle response. Browser smoke verifies handoff and reset. Added regression for traversing a settled body, real Rapier conversion, and benchmark with four living and four settled patients. Settling requires near-ground, lying geometry and low speed for 60 ticks, with a 12 s near-ground/lying fallback. The four anatomical groups and radius padding are provisional approximations. Performance and visual acceptance pending.
